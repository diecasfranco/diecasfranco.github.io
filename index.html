About Me

I am a microbiologist with broad expertise in microbial ecology and host-associated microbiomes, with over 20 years of research experience across five countries. My work spans Antarctic and marine sediment microbiology, insect endosymbiosis, clinical microbiome studies, and environmental microbiology in tropical and semi-arid ecosystems.

A major focus of my research is insect-associated microbiomes. In planthoppers (Hemiptera: Fulgoromorpha), I have investigated the genomics and transmission of nutritional endosymbionts across a diversity of host lineages. In Myrmica ants, I contributed to uncovering cryptic Spiroplasma co-infections involving two distinct clades, revealing caste-dependent infection heterogeneity and phylogenomic evidence for an exceptionally stable, maternally acquired symbiosis with a predicted persistence of seven million years. I also worked on the isolation of Lactic Acid Bacteria from Messor ants — particularly Weissella — sequencing their genomes and assessing their metabolic potential as probiotics. Currently, I work on honeybee (Apis mellifera) gut microbiomes, applying 16S amplicon sequencing and custom bioinformatics pipelines to understand how diet, environment, and colony management shape microbial communities.

I apply 16S/ITS amplicon sequencing, shotgun metagenomics, comparative genomics, and long-read sequencing platforms (PacBio and Oxford Nanopore) to characterise microbial communities and symbiont genomes at high resolution. I also develop open-source Python and Bash tools that make complex multi-sample analyses accessible through self-contained, reproducible workflows.
